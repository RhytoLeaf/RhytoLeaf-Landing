Best Ways to Learn the Time Value of Money

An honest look at EconoFlo, Khan Academy, Investopedia, and WAMLI — interactive vs. video vs. text, what each is good for, and where each falls short.

TL;DR

EconoFlo is the free, in-browser option where you do the math by playing with it — change a rate, a term, a payment, and watch the curve move. Khan Academy and WAMLI are better if you want structured video lessons. Investopedia is better as a reference to read. The BA II Plus wins when a teacher demands a calculator. They all solve different parts of the problem.

Tool by tool

EconoFlo Free

The experiment-first option. A free, browser-based companion that teaches economics fundamentals — TVM and IRR front and center — by letting you drag the numbers and watch the result change live.

  • Strengths: instant visual feedback; no account, no ads, no paywall; runs on any device; built by RhytoLeaf.
  • Weaknesses: it's a lab, not a syllabus — there's no guided course path or exam prep, and it won't replace a textbook for a full curriculum.

Khan Academy — Finance & Capital Markets Free

The structured self-study option. Free video lessons and practice questions from a well-known non-profit. If you want to work through economics the way you'd work through a course, this is the default.

  • Strengths: complete curriculum, free, huge library, practice with feedback.
  • Weaknesses: passive by design — you watch, you answer questions; the numbers never move until you open a calculator separately.

Investopedia Free + ads Premium subscription

The reference option. The place to look up what an IRR actually means. Articles and glossary entries are excellent; deeper courses sit behind a paid subscription.

  • Strengths: depth, credibility, searchable definitions, free tier.
  • Weaknesses: text-only — reading a formula is not the same as feeling it; ads on the free tier; paid for full courses.

WAMLI Free

The personal-finance video option. Well-regarded free video courses on money and markets, aimed at building everyday financial literacy rather than classroom economics.

  • Strengths: practical, engaging, genuinely free, no account needed.
  • Weaknesses: oriented to personal finance decisions, not TVM mechanics; no interactive tools.

Which should you use?

Learning a course? Start with Khan Academy's structured path, and keep EconoFlo open in a second tab — every time a lesson mentions compounding or discounting, reproduce it in the simulator. That loop (watch → experiment) is where the intuition actually forms.

Just need to understand a number? Open EconoFlo directly. Type in the loan, the rate, the years, and move one variable at a time.

Lost on a term? Investopedia's glossary, then come back to see it in motion.

Exam requiring a BA II Plus? Buy the calculator. No app — free or otherwise — shows up on a locked-down exam hall.
